Days a year with 50 mm of rain or more

Map: SpatialStat, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 Rain: ERA5, Copernicus Climate Change Service / ECMWF (CC BY 4.0) Imagery: NASA Blue Marble Borders: Natural Earth

For every cell of the ERA5 grid (0.25°, about 28 km) we counted the days a year when 50 mm of rain or more fell (snow and hail counted as water), then averaged the yearly counts over 2000–2010 and over 2016–2025. The map shows those averages on a 0.5° grid. Days run from midnight to midnight UTC.

“Change” is the second period minus the first. A change is shown bright when it is larger than normal year-to-year variation (Welch's t-test on the yearly counts, p < 0.05) and faint when it is not. Even with no real change, about one cell in twenty would pass this test by chance, so look for whole regions rather than single cells.

The current year is shown on its own and only for complete months (ERA5 runs about five days behind real time). It is compared with the same months in the earlier years, not with whole years.

ERA5 is a climate reanalysis: a weather model combined with observations, not a rain gauge. Its 28 km cells smooth out the heaviest local downpours, so it counts fewer extreme days than gauges and radars do, especially in thunderstorms and on mountains. Treat the numbers as a consistent comparison between periods, not as local records.
